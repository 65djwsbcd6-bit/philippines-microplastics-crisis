01. Classification

Plastic Scale & Degradation

Macroplastics (> 5 mm)

Visible ocean debris including single-use sachet wrappers, shopping bags, beverage bottles, and lost fishing nets adrift across coastal waters and coral reefs.

Microplastics (< 5 mm)

Particles created when larger plastics weather under solar UV light and mechanical wave action, alongside manufactured microbeads from cosmetics and textiles.

Nanoplastics (< 1 µm)

Ultra-fine microscopic fragments small enough to breach cellular membranes, cross intestinal barriers in marine life, and bioaccumulate in animal organs.

02. Data & Scale

Current Status in the Philippines

03. Drivers

Root Causes of Contamination

The Sachet Economy

Socioeconomic pressures drive widespread consumer reliance on small, single-serving multi-layer sachets for daily needs like shampoo, coffee, and seasonings.

Waste Recovery Infrastructure

Gaps in municipal waste collection, sorting facilities, and landfill capacity result in uncollected trash being swept into urban waterways during heavy monsoon rains.

04. Consequences

Ecological & Human Hazards

Marine Species Ingestion

Sea turtles, whale sharks, and fish mistake floating plastics for food, causing physical intestinal blockages, organ perforation, and eventual starvation.

Food Web Bioaccumulation

Microplastics consumed by marine zooplankton travel up oceanic food chains into commercial fish species harvested for local fish markets.

Human Health Exposure

Microplastic particles absorb toxic chemical compounds and heavy metals in seawater, carrying endocrine disruptors into human food supplies.
