Section 01 / Physical Transformation

WHAT HAPPENS TO PLASTIC AFTER WE THROW IT AWAY?

Unlike organic waste which biodegrades naturally into organic matter, synthetic polymers photodegrade. Sunlight ultraviolet radiation and mechanical wave action fragment larger objects into smaller particles.

Section 03 / Drivers & Systemic Drivers

WHY IS PLASTIC EVERYWHERE?

Plastic pollution is not merely an individual consumer issue. It is driven by socioeconomic retail packaging models, rapid food delivery systems, e-commerce, and infrastructure gaps.

01 / Beverage Utensils

Plastic Straws

Used briefly during cold drinks, plastic straws rarely get recycled due to light weight and small size, easily washing through drainage channels.

Philippine Impact: High daily usage in street refreshments and iced coffee stalls nationwide.
02 / Takeout Cutlery

Disposable Spoons & Forks

Single-use polystyrene and polypropylene cutlery bundled with fast food meals provide brief convenience but last centuries in landfills.

Philippine Impact: Included automatically in millions of daily carinderia and fast food delivery packages.
03 / Carrier Packaging

Single-Use Plastic Carrier Bags

Thin film plastic bags ("sando bags") clog drainage grids during heavy typhoons, directly aggravating urban flash floods.

Philippine Impact: Heavily used in local wet markets (palengke) for seafood and fresh produce.
04 / Sachet Economy

The Tingi / Sachet Market Model

Multinationals package shampoo, coffee, seasoning, and laundry detergent in small, non-recyclable multi-layer aluminum-plastic sachets targeted at daily-wage earners.

Systemic Factor: Millions of neighborhood sari-sari stores rely on sachets to offer budget-friendly single-use products.
05 / Urban Delivery

Food Delivery Packages

A single meal order often includes a plastic clam-shell container, outer carrier bag, beverage cup, lid, straw, and condiment sachets.

Visual Count: 1 Single Order = Up to 7 distinct single-use plastic components.
06 / E-Commerce Growth

Convenience Comes With Packaging

Online shopping platforms deliver thousands of items daily wrapped in multiple protective layers: courier plastic pouches, multi-layer bubble wrap, plastic stretch films, and synthetic adhesive tapes.

Section 04 / Ecological Impact

WHERE DOES THE PLASTIC END UP?

Once entering the natural environment, plastic affects ecosystems across all layers of the biosphere—from inland rivers and coastal mangroves to delicate coral reefs and marine life.

🌊 Rivers & Estuaries

Urban Inland Waterways

Floating waste accumulates in stagnant river choke points, blocking natural flow, smothering benthic habitats, and reducing dissolved oxygen levels essential for freshwater species.

🏖️ Coastal Shorelines

Pristine vs. Polluted Coasts

Even remote island beaches receive tide-borne microplastics and macro waste, threatening coastal ecotourism, nesting sites, and shoreline ecological stability.

🌱 Mangrove Forests

Mangrove Root Trapping

Dense prop roots of Philippine mangroves trap floating plastic bags and sheets, suffocating pneumatophores (breathing roots) and damaging vital fish nursery habitats.

🐢 Marine Wildlife

Ingestion & Entanglement

Sea turtles confuse floating translucent plastic bags with jellyfish. Ingested plastics block digestive tracts, leading to starvation, while ghost nets entangle fish and cetaceans.

🪸 Coral Reef Ecosystems

Reef Abrasion & Disease Transmission

Plastics snagged on coral heads cause physical tissue abrasion, block sunlight needed for zooxanthellae photosynthesis, and increase likelihood of coral disease outbreaks up to 20-fold.

Critical for Philippine coastal fisheries and marine biodiversity.

The Degradation Illusion

WHAT HAPPENS TO PLASTIC AFTER WE THROW IT AWAY?

Unlike organic matter which biodegrades into nutrient-rich soil, synthetic polymers never truly digest in nature. Solar radiation and mechanical erosion merely fragment larger debris into microscopic persistent particles.

🗑️ Macro Plastic Pollution

Plastic pollution occurs when discarded synthetic items accumulate in natural habitats, obstructing drainage networks, suffocating wildlife, and releasing chemical additives into coastal soils and rivers.

🔬 And What Are Microplastics?

Microplastics are microscopic plastic particles smaller than 5 millimeters (about the size of a sesame seed). They stem from primary manufacturing beads or secondary breakdown of larger items.

Everyday Sources & Drivers

WHY IS PLASTIC EVERYWHERE?

Modern conveniences and micro-retail economies have made single-use non-biodegradable packaging an omnipresent fixture in daily routines.

🥤 Straws

Plastic Straws

Used briefly for cold beverages and soft drinks, disposable plastic straws are easily lightweight enough to blow out of waste containers and enter local drainage systems.

🍴 Utensils

Disposable Spoons & Forks

A single takeout meal often involves multiple polystyrene or polypropylene utensils designed for single meals but surviving in landfills for centuries.

🛍️ Plastic Bags

Single-Use Carrying Bags

Thin-film plastic bags provided at wet markets and retail stalls easily tear, lack resale value for waste collectors, and wash out to sea.

🧴 The Sachet Economy

Multi-Layered Sachets (Tingi Culture)

Low-income households in the Philippines rely heavily on low-cost, single-serving sachets for everyday goods like shampoo, coffee, seasoning, and detergent.

Because sachets are composed of laminated plastic and aluminum film layers, they are virtually impossible to recycle, making up a massive percentage of Philippine shoreline waste.

⚡ Millions of individual sachets enter municipal streams every day across local sari-sari stores.
🍱 Food Delivery

Takeout Food Packaging

A single meal order incorporates plastic boxes, sauce cups, plastic wrap, outer bags, and cutlery, creating an immediate pile of single-use debris.

Ecosystem Degradation

WHERE DOES THE PLASTIC END UP?

Once plastic escapes human infrastructure, it spreads into critical Philippine ecological zones, altering delicate balances in water, shorelines, mangroves, and marine wildlife.

🌊 OUR WATER

Rivers & Freshwater Outfalls

Floating debris choking urban rivers reduces dissolved oxygen levels and releases toxic chemical plasticizers into surrounding freshwater bodies.

🏖️ OUR BEACHES

Coastal Beaches & Shorelines

Wave action deposits macro waste directly along tourist beaches and coastal fishing villages, creating severe environmental and economic impacts.

🌱 MANGROVES

Mangrove Forests

Dense mangrove root systems trap floating plastic bags and bottles, suffocating pneumatic roots essential for nursery fish species and storm surge defense.

🐢 OUR WILDLIFE

Marine Turtles & Cetaceans

Sea turtles mistake floating plastic bags for jellyfish, leading to fatal gastrointestinal blockages, internal injury, and starvation.

🪸 CORAL REEFS

Coral Ecosystems

Entangled plastic items abrade living coral polyps, creating entry points for pathogen infections that destroy rich benthic reef habitats.

🍽️ FOOD WEBS

Seafood & Human Exposure

Ingested microplastics bioaccumulate from plankton into pelagic fish species, ultimately returning to human dinner tables via commercial fisheries.
