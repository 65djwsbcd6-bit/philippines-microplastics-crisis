Section 01 / Physical Transformation[cite: 1]

WHAT HAPPENS TO PLASTIC AFTER WE THROW IT AWAY?[cite: 1]

Unlike organic waste which biodegrades naturally into organic matter, synthetic polymers photodegrade[cite: 1]. Sunlight ultraviolet radiation and mechanical wave action fragment larger objects into smaller particles[cite: 1].

Microscopic Stage[cite: 1]

Ecosystem Ingestion[cite: 1]

Trophic Marine Ingestion[cite: 1]

Plankton, small fish, and filter feeders ingest floating micro-particles mistaking them for food[cite: 1].

Section 03 / Drivers & Systemic Drivers[cite: 1]

WHY IS PLASTIC EVERYWHERE?[cite: 1]

Plastic pollution is not merely an individual consumer issue[cite: 1]. It is driven by socioeconomic retail packaging models, rapid food delivery systems, e-commerce, and infrastructure gaps[cite: 1].

01 / Beverage Utensils[cite: 1]

Plastic Straws[cite: 1]

Used briefly during cold drinks, plastic straws rarely get recycled due to light weight and small size, easily washing through drainage channels[cite: 1].

Philippine Impact:[cite: 1] High daily usage in street refreshments and iced coffee stalls nationwide[cite: 1].
02 / Takeout Cutlery[cite: 1]

Disposable Spoons & Forks[cite: 1]

Single-use polystyrene and polypropylene cutlery bundled with fast food meals provide brief convenience but last centuries in landfills[cite: 1].

Philippine Impact:[cite: 1] Included automatically in millions of daily carinderia and fast food delivery packages[cite: 1].
03 / Carrier Packaging[cite: 1]

Single-Use Plastic Carrier Bags[cite: 1]

Thin film plastic bags ("sando bags") clog drainage grids during heavy typhoons, directly aggravating urban flash floods[cite: 1].

Philippine Impact:[cite: 1] Heavily used in local wet markets (palengke) for seafood and fresh produce[cite: 1].
04 / Sachet Economy[cite: 1]

The Tingi / Sachet Market Model[cite: 1]

Multinationals package shampoo, coffee, seasoning, and laundry detergent in small, non-recyclable multi-layer aluminum-plastic sachets targeted at daily-wage earners[cite: 1].

Systemic Factor:[cite: 1] Millions of neighborhood sari-sari stores rely on sachets to offer budget-friendly single-use products[cite: 1].
05 / Urban Delivery[cite: 1]

Food Delivery Packages[cite: 1]

A single meal order often includes a plastic clam-shell container, outer carrier bag, beverage cup, lid, straw, and condiment sachets[cite: 1].

Visual Count:[cite: 1] 1 Single Order = Up to 7 distinct single-use plastic components[cite: 1].
06 / E-Commerce Growth[cite: 1]

Convenience Comes With Packaging[cite: 1]

Online shopping platforms deliver thousands of items daily wrapped in multiple protective layers: courier plastic pouches, multi-layer bubble wrap, plastic stretch films, and synthetic adhesive tapes[cite: 1].

Courier Pouch[cite: 1] Bubble Wrap[cite: 1] Plastic Tape[cite: 1] Internal Cushioning[cite: 1]
Section 04 / Ecological Impact[cite: 1]

WHERE DOES THE PLASTIC END UP?[cite: 1]

Once entering the natural environment, plastic affects ecosystems across all layers of the biosphere—from inland rivers and coastal mangroves to delicate coral reefs and marine life[cite: 1].

🌊 Rivers & Estuaries[cite: 1]

Urban Inland Waterways[cite: 1]

Floating waste accumulates in stagnant river choke points, blocking natural flow, smothering benthic habitats, and reducing dissolved oxygen levels essential for freshwater species[cite: 1].

🏖️ Coastal Shorelines[cite: 1]

Pristine vs. Polluted Coasts[cite: 1]

Even remote island beaches receive tide-borne microplastics and macro waste, threatening coastal ecotourism, nesting sites, and shoreline ecological stability[cite: 1].

🌱 Mangrove Forests[cite: 1]

Mangrove Root Trapping[cite: 1]

Dense prop roots of Philippine mangroves trap floating plastic bags and sheets, suffocating pneumatophores (breathing roots) and damaging vital fish nursery habitats[cite: 1].

🐢 Marine Wildlife[cite: 1]

Ingestion & Entanglement[cite: 1]

Sea turtles confuse floating translucent plastic bags with jellyfish[cite: 1]. Ingested plastics block digestive tracts, leading to starvation, while ghost nets entangle fish and cetaceans[cite: 1].

🪸 Coral Reef Ecosystems[cite: 1]

Reef Abrasion & Disease Transmission[cite: 1]

Plastics snagged on coral heads cause physical tissue abrasion, block sunlight needed for zooxanthellae photosynthesis, and increase likelihood of coral disease outbreaks[cite: 1].

Critical for Philippine coastal fisheries and marine biodiversity[cite: 1].
