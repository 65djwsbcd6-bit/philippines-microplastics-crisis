01. Conceptual Framework

Definition & Classification

Macroplastics

Debris greater than 5 mm. Includes single-use sachet films, bottles, and abandoned ghost fishing nets damaging Philippine coral reefs.

Microplastics

Particles smaller than 5 mm. Formed either as primary manufactured microbeads or secondary fragments weathered by UV rays and ocean waves.

Nanoplastics

Particles under 1 micrometer ($<1\ \mu\text{m}$). Capable of penetrating intestinal walls, entering bloodstream systems, and accumulating in organ tissues.

03. Drivers

Root Causes

Sachet Economy

Multi-layer non-recyclable plastic sachets targeted at low-income consumer habits across local barangays.

Infrastructure Deficits

Insufficient waste recovery, sorting facilities, and landfills in coastal municipal LGUs.

Synthetic Microfibers

Polyester garment washing discharges hundreds of thousands of microfibers into municipal waterways per wash cycle.

Tire Wear & Runoff

Frictional rubber wear and industrial nurdles washed into drains during heavy rainstorms and typhoons.

04. Ecological & Human Impact

Consequences and Hazards

Marine Mortality

Ingestion by sea turtles, whale sharks, and fish causes internal blockage, starvation, and habitat decay.

Food Web Transfer

Microplastics accumulate in plankton, moving up marine food chains to commercial fish harvested for consumption.

Human Toxicity

Chemical additives like BPA and phthalates act as endocrine disruptors when ingested via seafood and tap water.
