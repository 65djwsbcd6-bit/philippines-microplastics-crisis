01. Classification

Plastic Scale & Degradation

Macroplastics (> 5 mm)

Visible ocean debris including single-use sachet wrappers, shopping bags, plastic bottles, and lost fishing nets adrift in marine sanctuaries.

Microplastics (< 5 mm)

Particles resulting from wave friction, UV weathering, or primary manufactured microbeads in cosmetics and industrial nurdles.

Nanoplastics (< 1 µm)

Ultra-fine particles small enough to penetrate cellular membranes, translocate into fish tissue, and enter food chains.

02. Data & Scale

Current Status in the Philippines

Pasig River

Global River Plastic Contributor

Environmental studies identify Manila's Pasig River as one of the leading global riverways carrying urban plastic runoff into ocean bays.

Verde Passage

Biodiversity Center Threatened

Floating plastic micro-fragments accumulate in coastal waters surrounding the Verde Island Passage, threatening critical coral reefs.

03. Key Drivers

Root Causes of Plastic Runoff

Sachet Economy & Market Structure

Low-income purchasing habits encourage reliance on non-recyclable multi-layer sachets for everyday items (shampoo, coffee, seasonings).

Waste Recovery Infrastructure

Gaps in municipal waste segregation and landfill capacity lead to uncollected waste washing into drainage canals during typhoon rainstorms.

04. Consequences

Ecological & Human Health Impacts

Marine Mortality

Sea turtles and marine species mistake floating debris for jellyfish, causing intestinal blockage, toxicity, and starvation.

Food Web Bioaccumulation

Microparticles eaten by plankton transfer up marine food webs into commercial fish species harvested for consumer markets.

Human Health Risks

Ingested microplastics absorb heavy metals and persistent organic pollutants, posing endocrine disruptor risks.
